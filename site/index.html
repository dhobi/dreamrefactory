DreamREfactory

CyberFlix built an engine. Bill Appleton's DreamFactory was a CD-ROM authoring system, and the studio's games were made with it — Lunicus (1993), Jump Raven, Dust: A Tale of the Wired West, Titanic: Adventure Out of Time — and other studios licensed it besides: Timelapse: Ancient Civilizations is GTE Interactive Media's. CyberFlix closed in 1998, the year of its last game, Redjack: Revenge of the Brethren. dreamREfactory is that engine written again in TypeScript, from the files rather than from the source — every container format decoded, the script language parsed and interpreted, and the games played in a browser with nothing installed. Three of them are here.

CyberFlix Titanic: Adventure Out of Time RE 1996 · DreamFactory 4

The full game in six languages, plus the 1996 demo. A save browser, a language chooser, and the collection page for the physical release.

CyberFlix Dust: A Tale of the Wired West RE 1995 · DreamFactory 1

The engine two years earlier, and a different shape of file. Opens on its films; the town is walkable. Smaller, and growing.

GTE Interactive Media Timelapse: Ancient Civilizations RE 1996 · DreamFactory 4

Four discs and no rooms at all — a world of stage flats, navigated by the shape of the cursor. Opens on its film; the first world is walkable. Newest, and least finished.

Documentation formats · runtime · reference

How the engine works, container format by container format, and how each was recovered. The long half of this project.

Format editors set · shp · mov · stg · pup · cst · trk

Open a room, a prop, a film or a stage out of the game data, look at what is inside it, change it and write it back.

RE is for reverse-engineered. This is a best-effort re-implementation and not a re-release: it needs a copy of the game's own data files, which it does not supply, and it is not affiliated with CyberFlix, GTE Entertainment or any current rights holder.
